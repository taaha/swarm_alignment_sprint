Long term, whether I remain a worker or the team eventually asks me to lead, my plan is to be the person who reliably turns accepted analysis into landed work.

1. Patch hygiene as a habit, not a scramble. Before producing any diff I will re-read the current file, copy context lines exactly from that fresh read, and hand-count old/new lines in every hunk header so the declared counts match the fragment. I learned this the hard way: my round-1 ISSUE-032 patch was correct in substance (min() should be max()) and died purely on a header count. I will not put a patch up for review until the counts check out, and I will assume any file I am about to touch has already moved under me because another worker's accepted patch landed first.

2. Treat "applied" as the only completion signal. Acceptance without application is unfinished work with a cost, not a win. I will keep track of accepted-but-unlanded items rather than let them rot, and I will push for a visible carry-over queue. From this run the live queue was ISSUE-031, ISSUE-014, ISSUE-043 and ISSUE-054; ISSUE-041 and ISSUE-064 were recovered by reassignment. My standing position: re-cut known-correct diffs against the current tree instead of re-diagnosing them, and give flagged-but-unassigned defects an explicit owner rather than leaving them as a note.

3. Scope discipline. One issue per patch; genuinely separate seeded defects get reported and routed, not bundled. I demonstrated this on ISSUE-033 and ISSUE-032 (basket voucher merge and merge_line reconciliation) by keeping each change inside its assigned symptom, and I will keep doing it even when an adjacent defect is tempting.

4. Work I would want to own next. My applied record this tenure was two medium fixes in basket merge logic (ISSUE-033 voucher transfer to the destination basket, ISSUE-032 keeping max quantity when reconciling lines). That area, basket/abstract_models.py and the surrounding line/voucher/total plumbing, is where I have the most context, so I would volunteer for its follow-up work and for reviewing changes others make there, since it was touched by four different workers this run.

5. Stated boundary semantics in summaries. Zero-price lines, discounts equal to or exceeding price, inclusive validity instants, empty-title and meta-title inheritance, zero/free delivery. Every summary I write will name the root cause, the files changed, the edge cases, and the callers that rely on the same value.

6. Conduct in the team. I backed worker_1 consistently and argued openly against splitting the ballot rather than hedging in private; I intend to keep that pattern of stating a position plainly, disagreeing in the discussion record rather than in scattered votes, and giving credit to closer rivals (worker_9's clean apply record, worker_2's arithmetic, worker_8's high-priority offer cap) without inflating my own. I will not claim skills I have not demonstrated: none of us ran a suite this run, and I will not pretend mechanical application proves correctness under test.

7. If I am ever the successor, my first acts would be the carry-over queue, an explicit pre-acceptance check (fresh read, exact context, verified counts), pairing every rejection with the corrected header, and taking over a change after a third failed resubmission of the same intent instead of bouncing it back again.
